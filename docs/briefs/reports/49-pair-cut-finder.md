## In plain words
To read a two-syllable word, the game cuts it in two and reads each half on its own. Before, it cut at a pause, or else at the quietest moment. Now it first asks: "for this tone pair, where does Jane's join usually sit?" (for example 40% into the word for 1+2). It looks only near that spot: a pause, then a dip in loudness, then the pitch move typical for the pair (for 3+2: the low point). If it finds nothing there, it says "?" for both syllables. It never guesses. The word only decides **where** to cut. The halves are read without knowing which tones are wanted.

## Part 2: the low-only natural third tone (MEASURED, `t3low49.mjs`)
- **Premise gap:** none of Jane's 52 natural single third tones is low-only. In Praat all rise back by at least 2.4 semitones (median 6.5). So "Jane's natural low-only third tones ≥ 90%" has n = 0.
- Stand-in: the same 52 clips cut off at their low point (no rise back). Before: third tone **35 / 52 (67%)** on the ruler, 17 "none" (old board 50 / 52). Cause: the voice starts near line 2 and stops on the bottom line, so the fall is only 0.6–1.4 lines; the low-fall family scores under the 50% floor → "none".
- Fix (natural style, ruler reader only): a fall of ≥ 0.3 lines that ends on the bottom line (low ≤ 1.15) with no rise back reads third tone, never decisive. After: **50 / 52 (96%)**.
- No new wrong reads: Jane's full natural and textbook singles give exactly the same reads with the rule on and off (second tone: 44 right, 2 read T3, both runs).
- Pierre: his one low-only natural third tone (`shu3_2`, Praat rise 0.4 st) reads "second tone" in both runs; the tracker hears a small rise Praat does not. Not changed by this rule.

## Decisions for Pierre
1. **Natural pairs need a reader change, not a cut change.** Options: (a) keep "?" for short natural syllables (honest, today's state); (b) a short-syllable reader for pair halves under ~200 ms that uses height and slope (works on Jane; risky on a learner's scale that is off; never decisive); (c) read natural pairs as one shape against the pair's family (would use the target: breaks this brief's rule). My lean: (a) now, (b) as a measured follow-up on your natural takes.
2. **1+2 syllable 2 read as a third tone** (公園: textbook 60% both right). Jane's 園 dips deep before it rises; a close copy looks like a third tone to the reader. A reader fix for a second tone *after* another syllable (the dip comes from the join). Separate brief.
3. Do you want the stats to count pair syllables now that the 公園 T4 bug is gone? Pairs still never cost a heart.
